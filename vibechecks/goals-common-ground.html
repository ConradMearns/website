      One reading, not a finding. The goal hierarchy supplies the reference, symbols carry it between people, boundaries define who shares it, and sanctions correct deviation.       Candidate common ground

      Care for people close to youSafetyFairnessAutonomyRelatedness

      Benevolence, meaning care for close others, ranks near the top in Schwartz’s value surveys across cultures. Self-determination theory lists autonomy, competence and relatedness. Moral foundations work suggests care and fairness are endorsed across the spectrum but weighted differently, though that theory is contested. Tradition ranks low on average, yet the tradition versus openness-to-change axis is where political groups tend to differ most. The divergence is over whose circle counts and which risks matter.

      Needs, wants and interviewing for obstacles

      Several fields converge on one move: ask why to climb from a stated want to the need behind it, and ask how else to come back down to other ways of meeting it.

      Need
      Stay healthy and keep the family safe
      why? ↑↓ how else?
      Want or goal
      Stay warm
      why? ↑↓ how else?
      Satisfiers
      Build a fireWear a coatBuy blanketsShare a shelter

      People can share the need and the want while using different satisfiers. Conflicts tend to appear between satisfiers (a smoky fire) more than between needs, which is why common ground is often found one level up.

      A six-step interview sequence

      My synthesis of the methods behind the questions below, not a validated protocol. With several parties, interview each separately first, map interests side by side, look for shared needs with compatible satisfiers, and apply objective criteria.

        Question bank

        Pitfalls

        • Leading questions and your own frame can manufacture goals, and power asymmetry distorts what people say.
        • “Why” can invite justification, so “what” and “how” questions often work better.
        • Stated wants are weak evidence: wanting and liking come apart, people mispredict what will satisfy them, and they often confabulate their reasons.
        • Coaching has moderate effects in meta-analyses, but GROW, nonviolent communication and jobs-to-be-done have thin controlled evidence.

        Showing community around a shared need

        Suppose everyone is trying to stay warm and building fires. Keep the need and the satisfier on separate levels, count people, and tick cells to watch the measures change.

        Also worth reporting: importance times gap, conflicts between satisfiers (smoke against clean air), and saturation. Publishing the overlap creates common knowledge, which helps people coordinate. A convenience sample cannot estimate prevalence.

        Related methods

        Cautions for looped priorities

        • Fixed-point priorities can reflect graph structure, such as hub nodes, more than real importance.
        • Recursion over noisy inputs creates false precision, so use ranks or intervals and test sensitivity.
        • Use damped updates, since aggressive updating can oscillate or run away.
        • Take importance from behavior (usage, tickets) and trade-off surveys, not from self-rating.

        Aligning people: what exists beyond listening

        Reflective listening, where one person speaks and the other repeats without adding, is one technique among many. Sorting techniques by what they change shows where it fits and what else is available.

        Channel

        Can each side speak and be heard? Listening, motivational interviewing.

        Model

        What does each side believe about the facts and about the other side? Corrections, canvassing, prebunking.

        Reference

        What is each side trying to achieve, and is any goal shared? Common tasks, interest-based negotiation.

        Structure

        How does the group decide, and what gets amplified? Deliberation, bridging, facilitated methods.

        Where listening sits. Reflective listening repairs the channel. It usually has to come first, but repairing the channel does not by itself change what anyone is trying to achieve.

        Every lever here works in both directions. The knowledge of goals, identity and channels that supports alignment also supports manipulation, and the difference is largely transparency and consent. Influence operations, including the Russian concept of reflexive control, pull the same levers without consent.
