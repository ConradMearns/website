    The goal loop

    A control-loop reading of why some people finish and others stall. Choose a part of the loop to see what tends to break there, the levers researchers have tested, and how strong the evidence looks. The evidence labels are my rough summary, not a systematic review.

    strong many studies or a meta-analysis moderate replicated, modest or context-dependent mixed conflicting results contested replications disputed emerging few studies so far practitioner widely used, little controlled evidence

    Loops stack

    Be goals. Identity and values: “someone who keeps their word.” They set the reference for the level below.
    Do goals. Programs and projects: “finish the draft this week.”
    Motor goals. Movements and habits: “open the document at nine.”

    Each level’s output is the reference for the level below (Powers; Carver and Scheier). A rough rule of thumb, and my own gloss: failures near the top look like motivation problems, and failures near the bottom look like forgetting.

    From goals to group boundaries

    Five steps that connect the goal literature to identity, symbols and in-group control. Each step has sources; the chain itself is a synthesis, one way of assembling them.

      One reading, not a finding. The goal hierarchy supplies the reference, symbols carry it between people, boundaries define who shares it, and sanctions correct deviation. On this view, alignment work has to touch the reference (shared goals), the carrier (symbols) or the boundary (contact), not only the argument.

      Aligning people: what exists beyond listening

      Reflective listening, where one person speaks and the other repeats without adding, is one technique among many. Sorting the techniques by what they change shows where it fits and what else is on the table. Evidence labels are my rough summary.

      Channel

      Can each side speak and be heard? Listening, motivational interviewing.

      Model

      What does each side believe about the facts and about the other side? Corrections, canvassing, prebunking.

      Reference

      What is each side trying to achieve, and is any goal shared? Common tasks, interest-based negotiation.

      Structure

      How does the group decide, and what gets amplified? Deliberation, bridging, facilitated methods.

      Where listening sits. Reflective listening repairs the channel. It usually has to come first, but repairing the channel does not by itself change what anyone is trying to achieve.

      Every lever here works in both directions. The knowledge of goals, identity and channels that supports alignment also supports manipulation, and the difference is largely transparency and consent. Influence operations, including the Russian concept of reflexive control, pull the same levers without consent.
