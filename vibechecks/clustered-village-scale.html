Scale

The question the design has to answer before share means anything: at 10 clusters (30 agents), how much of the smith’s 64-bit working memory does one cluster cost on its own, and what happens when share clusters are told to meet at one square? Frame width at clusters=10, by share — the smith’s frame, the tightest of the three roles in the baseline. Two implementations of the same knob, because the naive reading of “generated by index” and a deliberately scoped one land in different places:

Requested at the top of this run: share = 1, 3, 6. At 3 and 6, both implementations overflow — the naive one immediately (share=2), the deliberately-scoped one by share=5. share is not free headroom to dial: it is a cliff, not a slope, because grounding reads objects within kHops of the shared square whether or not anyone is physically standing there this tick. A believed workplace two hops from a shared square is still two hops away — which means the survey’s own “an acquaintance not co-located costs 0 bits” finding does not transfer here: co-located and within-kHops are not the same test, and a share-group puts every member’s workplace inside kHops=2 by construction, every tick, whether anyone trades that tick or not.

Population, on its own, is exactly as cheap as the households anchor predicts. clusters from 3 to 100 at share=1 left the smith’s frame unchanged — still 29/64 bits, 24 ops — because ownMap plus a scenery() scoped to one cluster means a mind never hears about a cluster it doesn’t belong to. Wall time over a real start() run tracked the same story: 332.6 ms/agent at 3 clusters, 307.1 at 10, 303.2 at 30 — flat, the households shape, not the fort’s. This design sits squarely on the households side of the ladder: share is the fort’s hub risk reintroduced on purpose, and clusters alone is free the way isolated corners always are.
